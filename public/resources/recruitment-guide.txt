FUTURE FOUNDERS — FIND YOUR FIRST TEN
Draft chapter resource

Start with conversations, not a follower count.

1. Write a clear invitation: “We’re getting students together to build projects and test business ideas. Want to try a build session?”
2. Ask each founding team member to invite two people personally.
3. Look beyond business clubs. Designers, engineers, writers, organizers, and curious beginners all bring useful skills.
4. Ask existing student organizations to share your school-approved meeting notice.
5. Explain exactly what will happen at the first meeting. Give a date, a place, and one useful activity.
6. Make the first session easy to join without preparation.
7. Follow your school’s rules for collecting contact details. Do not add people to messages without permission.
8. Send a clear next step through an approved channel.
9. Ask returning members what made them come back.
10. Make participation worthwhile before trying to make the group bigger.
